The Commission

Your vision. A shared process. A car of your own.

You may begin with a detailed brief or little more than a feeling. The process moves from initial ideas toward a well-considered automobile, one decision at a time.

Stage 01

Conversation

Discover the customer's vision, needs, tastes, and ambitions.

You bring
Your ideas, however formed: a feeling, a use, a reference.
We contribute
We listen, ask, and offer first thoughts.
It establishes
A shared understanding of what the car is for.
Decision
Which priorities matter most.

Stage 02

Direction

Explore possibilities and develop a shared creative direction.

You bring
Reactions: what feels right, what doesn't.
We contribute
Sketches, material references, alternatives.
It establishes
A creative direction you both believe in.
Decision
Which direction to develop.

Stage 03

Development

Evaluate design and engineering options, resolve constraints, and refine the proposal.

You bring
Your priorities when trade-offs appear.
We contribute
Engineering judgment; honest explanation of constraints.
It establishes
A proposal that is feasible, not just attractive.
Decision
How to resolve conflicts between wishes.

Stage 04

Specification

Agree on the details, materials, scope, and intended outcome.

You bring
Review and approval.
We contribute
A clear specification, with open questions stated.
It establishes
Agreed scope and intended outcome.
Decision
Whether to approve before work proceeds.

Stage 05

Creation

Develop the automobile through coordinated design, engineering, specialist manufacturing, and integration.

You bring
Availability for meaningful updates.
We contribute
Coordination, integration, and quality oversight.
It establishes
A car taking shape, with you kept informed.
Decision
Any change in direction, with its implications explained first.

Stage 06

Handover

Present the finished vehicle and establish the continuing ownership relationship.

You bring
Your readiness to take the car.
We contribute
A personal presentation and separate documentation.
It establishes
The start of ownership, with your AD alongside.
Decision
How you'd like ongoing support to work.

Your AD

One person who knows the commission.

Each customer has a dedicated AD, their primary point of contact throughout the commission and into ownership.

Your AD understands the commission, maintains continuity, coordinates communication, and helps you navigate decisions, so you never repeat your vision to different departments.

Changing your mind

A change in direction is a conversation, not a reason to feel guilty.

You can change your mind as a commission develops. We won't charge an arbitrary fee simply because you wish to reconsider.

Changes can have real consequences, though, depending on the stage: additional design work, new materials, revised engineering, or changes to work already underway. Where a change has cost or schedule implications, we explain them first. Additional chargeable work proceeds only once you understand the consequences and approve the revised direction.

The distinction is between arbitrary penalties and the legitimate cost of real work.

Common questions

Before you begin.

Do I need a finished design?

No. You can arrive with detailed sketches or with little more than a feeling about how the car should be. We start from there.

Will you tell me what is and isn't feasible?

Yes. Where an idea conflicts with engineering, safety, or practicality, we explain why and offer alternatives rather than simply saying no.

Do I need to share a budget to start?

No. Budget can be discussed when it becomes useful, once the direction is taking shape.

What if my ideas change?

They can. We explain any real cost or schedule implications before additional chargeable work proceeds.

Personal elements of the experience

Details that support the car.

  • The commissioning letterWhen a commission is formally agreed, you receive a physical letter introducing your AD and explaining the next steps.
  • The design archiveA physical commissioning book: a curated record of selected design work, approved decisions, material selections, and the car's development.
  • The scale modelThe completed automobile is accompanied by an accurately developed scale model.

At handover, the car stays central. Engineering documentation, maintenance information, and ownership records are presented separately and clearly.
